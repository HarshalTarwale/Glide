# CRM

Two screens under **Leads** and **Pipeline** in the sidebar, plus a
follow-up log you'll see on both.

## Leads

A lead is someone you haven't sold to yet — captured before you know if
they're a real opportunity.

**New lead**: name, company, email, phone, and where they came from
(website, referral, cold outreach, event, advertising). It starts as
**New**; move it through **Contacted** → **Qualified** yourself as you work
it, or **Unqualified** if it's not going anywhere.

**Converting a lead**: once you're ready, click **Convert** on the lead's
own page. This does two things at once:

1. Creates a real **Contact**, marked as a customer, from the lead's name/company/email/phone
2. Optionally opens an **Opportunity** against that new contact — give it a name, an expected value, and an expected close date, or skip this if you just want the contact without tracking a specific deal yet

A converted lead is kept, not deleted — it's your record of where that
customer actually came from. Its status can't be changed again after
conversion, and it can't be converted a second time.

## Pipeline

A Kanban board — one column per stage (New → Qualified → Proposal →
Negotiation → Won / Lost), each card showing the deal's expected value and
probability. The header shows your open pipeline value, the same total
weighted by each deal's probability (a $1M deal at 5% barely moves this
number — a $50K deal at 75% moves it a lot more), and your win rate once
you've closed at least one deal.

**Moving a deal forward**: use the stage dropdown on its card, or open the
deal and use the stepper at the top. Moving to a new stage resets its
probability to that stage's typical value (10% New, 25% Qualified, 50%
Proposal, 75% Negotiation) — you can still edit that later if a specific
deal is more or less likely than the default.

**Closing a deal**: **Mark won** needs nothing else. **Mark lost** requires
a reason — it's the one fact worth keeping about a deal that didn't close.

**Creating an opportunity directly** (without going through a lead): **New
opportunity**, pick an existing contact, set the expected value.

## Follow-ups

Both a lead's page and an opportunity's page have a follow-up log: log a
call, email, meeting, or to-do with a due date, and check it off once it's
done. This isn't a calendar or an email integration — it's a simple record
of what you did and what's still open, the same as a paper follow-up list.
